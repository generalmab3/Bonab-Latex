{\bfseries\fontsize{20pt}{28pt}\selectfont Abstract\par}
\vspace{1.1cm}

Energy management in a microgrid means deciding, at each hour, how to serve the load, whether to charge or discharge the battery, and how much electricity to buy from or sell to the utility grid. The decisions are not only economic. The powers must remain balanced and the battery state of charge must stay inside an allowed interval. A neural network trained only on examples may follow the labels and still break those rules. Classical optimization usually respects the constraints more firmly, but solving the program again and again is not always cheap.

This project uses a physics-informed neural network. Residuals of power balance and of battery charge dynamics are added to the same objective that trains the network, together with the usual data error. The operating variables follow grid-connected energy-management models. Profit sharing among several sites, which some papers treat with game theory, is not part of the numerical test.

A small microgrid with photovoltaic generation, a battery and a local load was studied. The data came from a twenty-one-day hourly simulator with a fixed random seed. A feed-forward network was programmed in Python. On the test hours the physics-informed model reduced the power-balance residual and the rate of charge-limit violations relative to a data-only network, while the mean squared error increased slightly. The finding holds only for this synthetic set and does not claim superiority in the field.
