{\bfseries\fontsize{20pt}{28pt}\selectfont Abstract\par}
\vspace{1.1cm}

Energy management in a microgrid is a constrained decision problem: local demand must be served, renewable generation should be used effectively, the storage device must remain within admissible limits, and exchange with the utility grid must remain physically and economically meaningful. Classical optimization can enforce these constraints explicitly, yet repeated solution over long horizons or in online settings may be expensive. Purely data-driven neural networks infer quickly, but they do not, by themselves, guarantee power balance or storage limits.

This undergraduate project combines two research streams in a controlled way. The first is the physics-informed neural-network framework, in which governing equations enter the training objective. The second is the operational formulation of a grid-connected microgrid, including power variables, storage dynamics, grid exchange, and, at a higher layer, fair profit allocation among participants. The contribution is not a literal translation of those papers. It is a compact, implementable model that mixes a data residual with power-balance, state-of-charge, bound-penalty, and optional economic terms.

The baseline system contains photovoltaic generation, a battery, a local load, and bidirectional exchange with the main grid. A feed-forward network is proposed and a Python/PyTorch implementation path is specified. The game-theoretic layer is treated as an optional extension for profit sharing, not as a completed numerical claim of the present version.

The report therefore supplies the theoretical frame, the equations, the experimental protocol, and a code skeleton. No numerical finding is stated before a real experiment is run. That separation is also the correct stance for the oral defense.
